# It's not Age Verification. It's centralized control.

More and more governments are legislating "age vefification" with the surface level messaging of "protecting the kids". This post will go over how the surface level justification is a veil for imposing strict government surveillance and why it will lead to negative consequences for the average person. I'm going to get a bit hand wavy and hyperbolic at times, but I hope you can step back and see what risks make sense in your context.

For a less tech-focused overview of the state of age verification laws, check out [this article](https://tuta.com/blog/age-verification-kills-anonymity).

## Does it protect the kids?

The internet, and honestly the world at large is not a safe place for minors to be interacting with strangers. Most folks I know that grew up with the internet have had encounters where their lack of experience led to unpleasant situations.

The question is, would having their face scanned in order to asses their age have prevented these situations? I would say no and that doing so ends up being worse than nothing.

### Data leaks

One of the most obvious issues is that the companies collecting this data are prone to leaks with all that private information becoming available for sketchy groups to do what they will. Biometric data is especially sensitive, and doubly so for the most vulnerable. I think it's irresponsible to put kids at risk without materially serious consequences for both the breached conpanies and companies like Discord that force their users to hand over their data to them. Outside of kids this is a risk to anybody using these platforms.

### Bypassing can lead to worse outcomes

Roblox is a good example of how these controls can actually bring more harm. For one, the biometric based "age estimation" are [easy to bypass](https://mashable.com/article/roblox-age-verfication-system-fooled). On the other side, false positives could lead to adults being put into child only spaces and children being forced into adult only spaces. This is worse than doing nothing, or even doing the usual self-reporting of your age by taking a birth date which has been the standard approach everywhere else.

### Restricting Important Information

Even when the checks work, what information is actually being hidden from underage people can lead to problems. One big issue is that anything related to queer identities is being marked as being unsuitable for minors, thus leaving people lost and confused when their experience of the world doesn't align with what they're being told. A popular target is [anything to do with LGBTQ identities](https://www.equaldex.com/issue/censorship). Transgender people have existed [since history has been tracked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_history) and hiding this from people leads to [dissociation, depersonalization, and extreme damage to their mental health](https://www.reddit.com/r/asktransgender/comments/vtze2d/trans_people_who_have_repressed_their_identity/). Similarly, keeping your fruity kid unaware that gay people exist is only going to make them [suffer](https://www.nami.org/community-and-culture/lgbtq/trauma-and-internalized-shame/). Another area where censorship can impact youths badly is hiding information about sexual health. Although some parents might be worried about exposing their children to anything related to sexual intercourse, keeping it from them puts them at [higher risk of sexually transmitted infections](https://www.guttmacher.org/gpr/2020/04/reducing-sti-cases-young-people-deserve-better-sexual-health-information-and-services) and can put them at [higher risk of sexual violence](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/comprehensive-sexuality-education#accordion-panel-8).

## Surveillance and Censorship

What I'm trying to get at is that it isn't really about protecting the kids. Then what is it about? It's about surveillence and control. In the fight against queer people and ethnic minorities, "think of the kids" is an edge used to bypass people's rational thought to covertly push things that will end up harming them.

### Palantir

A smoking gun in this case is how Palantir [a company well known for doing evil shit](https://www.npr.org/2025/05/01/nx-s1-5372776/palantir-tech-contracts-trump) has their fingers [all over the "age verification" sector](https://kotaku.com/discord-palantir-peter-thiel-persona-age-verification-2000668951). They've been successful at getting their surveillence systems deployed globally, sucking up people's data, and using it to help governments (and who knows who else) to imprison and kill people. These are the sorts of groups that want to wedge more invasive surveillence into every possible place they can for the sake of disempowering people, and empowering those that seek to bring them harm.

### Governmental Information Control (derogatory)

Companies like Palantir go hand in hand with governments that want greater control over dissenters to their whims. This is especially relevant given that [upper eschelons of government end up being misaligned with their constituants needs](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/collective-representation-in-congress/D935F856F7BEA10A25442C4EF57675ED) combined with the amount of propaganda already being targetted at people to influence what those needs are. In the USA, ICE is being empowerd by [these tools](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jan/30/ice-surveillance-app-mobile-fortify-authoritarian) to [illegaly jail](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/courts-have-ruled-4400-times-that-ice-jailed-people-illegally-it-hasnt-stopped-2026-02-14/) people with little to no reprecussion. Online, governments around the world are activly [censoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_and_surveillance_by_country) content to limit people's ability to [protest or even know that protests are happening](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10953837/). This speaks to the holders of surveillence being misaligned with the needs of the people they have control over. The more we give in to these encroachments on privacy, the less power people will have to take care of each other when at the whims of those who wish them harm.

## Software Lockdown

Historically even though surveillence and censorship has been all around us, there was still a way to bypass it. At the end of the day computers and the internet are built to connect anyone to anything (with some restrictions) regardless of who they are. This is starting to change since governments and tech companies are starting to integrate legal identity as a core part of computing infrastructure. One recent example is California mandating age verification at the [operating system level](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/operating-systems/a-new-california-law-says-all-operating-systems-including-linux-need-to-have-some-form-of-age-verification-at-account-setup/). On it's own it isn't too strange since they're only requiring a date of birth and only allowing age "ranges" for applications to query against instead of the raw date. But this already erodes privacy by making it easier for trackers like advertisers to better identify who is on the other end and [how best to manipulate them](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374649104_Age_differences_in_adaption_of_persuasion_strategies_in_advertising). This change is also a stepping stone to more advanced identity verification like the [Google Credential Manager Verifier API](https://developer.android.com/identity/digital-credentials/credential-verifier), [Apple's Verifier API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ProximityReader/checking-ids-with-the-verifier-api), and the equivalents coming to [the web](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/digital-credentials-api-shipped). These are already being used to gatekeep access to services and we're just a few years away from everything being gatekept behind government verification.

For the super nerds in the crowd you might be thinking that you can simply choose not to use software that mandates government ID, but will that be allowed by [app stores](https://www.reddit.com/r/enshittification/comments/1oe9z61/google_now_requiring_age_verification_in_usa/) and will we have the option to run software [that hasn't been pre-approved](https://keepandroidopen.org/) and forced to comply? The trend seems to be going in the direction of locking down what software is allowed to run at all, and what developers must do to allow their software to be installable.

If these trends continue we could be going towards complete (digital) information control. For a preview of what it'll look like, check out what [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_Iran) has been doing with full control over internet traffic, and also blocking any app stores that don't comply and replacing them with locally controlled options. Instead of going to these lengths, governments will likely [just ask the major tech companies to lock things down directly](https://apnews.com/article/apple-ice-iphone-app-immigration-fb6a404d3e977516d66d470585071bcc) and citizens will have no other choice.

## Downsides for average people

On the consumer side this means that you will no longer have a chance at anonymity when using online services, which means that you'll be more vulnerable to advertisers, but also means that your access to all services can be terminated in one place and anything you post being easy to trace for when [ICE makes the rounds to send you to a camp](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/feb/17/ice-holds-people-in-disgusting-conditions-now-its-turning-warehouses-into-camps) for posting the [wrong meme](https://people.com/jd-vance-bald-meme-face-trolling-van-uk-11790518). You'll need to have this as part of your thought process when registering for any service or accessing any resources.

The companies charged with storing this information are notoriously [bad at it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_data_breaches) and we'll likely see a rise in [identity theft](https://www.reginapolice.ca/2025/11/06/identity-theft-fraud-and-counterfeit-incidents-up-78-in-2025/) as this data gets breached and as access to identity becomes a more valuable asset for interacting with information systems. Stealing your drivers license is bad now, but how bad will it be when criminals feel the need to collect IDs for acessing more benign internet services for their activities?

Even if you consider yourself to be a model citizen with nothing to hide and nothing bad to say about the government (or anyone with power), you're still at risk of false positives causing you harm. A great example today is how Google's centralized identity can cause you to [lose everything if an automated system flags you](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2022/aug/22/google-csam-account-blocked). With Government ID backed access control, you'd be losing acess to lose *literally everything*. Given the rise of [low quality LLM generated code](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026/02/28/when-ai-writes-the-worlds-software.html), we can expect people to fall through the cracks as bugs have high reprecussions. People complaining will fall into the "protestor" bucket and get more automated reprecussions. 

## Downsides for computer people

For companies, having a centralized point of identity can be nice since it makes compliance easier if that's a requirement. But outside of that immediate advantage, there will be a bunch of frictions to making and distributing useful information technology to people.

- Higher barrier to entry for creation
- Mandatory centralization with higher risk data management
- Some things just won't be allowed to exist

## What do we do about it?

Call your representatives, make a fuss, don't preemptively give in until you have no other choice. Stop paying for platforms that joyfully add this stuff without being forced. Go outside and find your local community of weirdos and see how you can take care of each other.
